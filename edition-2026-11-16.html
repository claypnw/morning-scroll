Around the Holds · The Week Ahead

Ostriches, First Stones, and Falcons Open the Week

Payson, Ariz.

Listen to this story

Hail, wayfarer, and good Morndas to you — pull up a bench by the hearth while the grey clouds hang low over the Rim. Payson starts the week with two gatherings at ten this morning: historian Janelle Molony tells how Arizona once held the world’s largest collection of ostriches at the Payson Public Library, and neighbors break ground for the new Banner Payson Cancer Center. Down the road in Sedona, falcons take the sky for World Falconry Day, and the days ahead bring a trail-building morning in Pine, a flag dedication, Prescott’s Festival of Trees, and Payson’s Turkey Trot on Saturday.

Happening in the Holds Today

Listen to this story

  • Payson— Historical Presentation — Arizona’s Ostrich Ranching History, 10:00 AM at the Payson Public Library, with the Library Friends of Payson. Historian Janelle Molony tells how Arizona once held the largest single collection of ostriches in the world, why other countries envied those birds, and where the great ranches stood. It comes right after the Friends’ 10 AM announcements at their November general meeting. Free, friendly, and perfect for history lovers and curious travelers alike.— Banner Payson Cancer Center Groundbreaking, 10:00 AM on the south side of the main Banner Payson building. Neighbors, caregivers, and town folk gather with golden shovels to mark the first stones of a new cancer center for Rim Country. The ceremony celebrates care closer to home, so Payson families will not have to ride so far down the mountain for treatment. Come cheer the start of a building this Hold has wanted for a long time. All are welcome to stand with the builders.— Softball Team B at Legacy QC, 4:00–7:30 PM, listed by Payson Unified School District at 902 W. Main Street. Young Longhorn athletes take the field for an afternoon contest, with families and friends in the stands. School sports are small-town theater at its best — cheers, dust, and pride in every inning. Bundle up against the grey breeze and bring a warm drink. A fine way to close a cool Morndas.
  • Sedona— World Falconry Day 2026 — A Falconer’s Journey: Stories from the Field, 4:00 PM in Sedona. Dr. Michele Losee, raptor ecologist, master falconer, and head of the International Raptor & Falconry Center, shares a behind-the-scenes look at life with trained birds of prey. Expect tales from the field rather than a dry lecture — the bond between falconer and bird, told by one who lives it. Bird lovers and the simply curious will both find wonder here. A rare chance to hear a true master of an ancient art.— Morning Sound Bath Session, 10:00 AM at Crystal Tones in Sedona. Start the day inside a ring of alchemy crystal singing bowls, with more than a hundred bowls sounding against the red rocks. The one-hour session is a quiet, restful pause — travelers call it a Sedona must-do for recharging body and mind. Arrive a little early to settle in. A gentle beginning to a grey Monday.

The Days Ahead

Listen to this story

  • Tue, Nov. 17— Trail Day Volunteer Build in Pine, 7:00 AM, with Pine Strawberry Fuel Reduction. Don’t just use the trail — help build it. Hikers, bikers, runners, and neighbors clear brush, move rocks, and shape new trail that will serve the twin villages for years. Bring gloves, water, and a willing back; the crew brings the know-how. Work done together under the pines tastes sweetest at lunch. A true Hold-building morning.— Wrestling vs. Show Low / Blue Ridge in Payson, 4:00–9:00 PM at 902 W. Main Street. Payson’s wrestlers meet two White Mountain rivals on the mat in one long evening of matches. Expect grit, quick pins, and a loud hometown crowd. Families and students fill the gym, and every bout carries school pride. Come lend your voice to the Longhorns.
  • Wed, Nov. 18— Rally Around the Flag Memorial Dedication, 10:00 AM at the Gila County complex in Payson. New flagpoles now carry the American flag, the Arizona flag, the six military branch flags, and the POW/MIA flag, and this ceremony raises them in dedication. Veterans, families, and neighbors are invited to stand together for the raising. The Rim Country Camera Club calls it a fine morning to meet fellow photographers, too. A solemn and proud hour for the Hold.— Rim Country Camera Club Meeting, 4:30–6:30 PM in the Payson Library large meeting room, 328 N. McLane Road. Photography lovers share a reel photo review and talk pictures with fellow shutter-keepers. Members may send up to three jpeg photos ahead for the review. New faces are welcome — bring your curiosity if not your camera. A warm room on a cool evening.
  • Thu, Nov. 19— Festival of Trees Prescott, November 19–21, 10:00 AM–8:00 PM daily at Sam Hill Warehouse, 232 N. Granite Street, Prescott. Rows of decorated Christmas trees and wreaths fill the old warehouse, with holiday vendors, live entertainment, visits with Santa, and crafts for children. It is Prescott’s joyful first step into the Christmas season. Valley Dwellers making the climb will find the hall merry and bright. Go early in the day for the easiest wandering.
  • Fri, Nov. 20— Charcuterie Make ’n Take — Holiday Season Edition in Payson, 6:00 PM. A hands-on class where you build your own Thanksgiving- and Christmas-inspired charcuterie board, learning arrangement tricks that make a board look like a feast. You take your creation home, ready to impress family and guests. Festive, tasty, and practical all at once. A delicious way to open the holiday season.— Salty Corner — Dry Bar Comedy in Payson, 7:00 PM. The last Salty Corner show of 2026 promises big laughs and good company in a clean-comedy night out. Bring friends, settle in, and end the year laughing together. Tickets were announced as free and coming soon, so check with the organizers before you ride out. A merry Fredas night in the Hold.
  • Sat, Nov. 21— Turkey Trot in Payson, 8:00 AM at Green Valley Park, 1000 W. Country Club Drive. The annual fun run awards prizes for fastest stroller, best themed outfit, and more, with event shirts while supplies last. Runners, walkers, and costumed trotters of every age are welcome. Sign up through Payson Parks and Recreation. The merriest way to earn your feast.— Payson Showdown, 11:00 AM in Payson. A hometown showdown gathering with vendors and food trucks rolling in for the day. Browse the booths, eat well, and visit with neighbors between events. The organizers sought vendors and food trucks through the Town of Payson this fall, so expect a lively field. A fine midday stop after the Turkey Trot.— Free To Be Me Drumming in Sedona, 12:00–2:00 PM at Posse Grounds Park, 525 Posse Ground Road. An open community drum circle where every hand is welcome and no experience is needed. Bring a drum if you have one, or simply bring yourself and keep the beat. Sunshine, red rocks, and rhythm make a joyful midday. Travelers and locals drum side by side.
  • Sun, Nov. 22— Women’s Descending & Drops Clinic in Sedona, 10:00 AM. Sedona’s famous downhill trails are the classroom for a clinic on descending and drops, led on terrain few places in the world can match. Riders will meet steep, technical features with coaching and good company. Bring your bike, your courage, and your questions. A bold Sundas for women who ride.

The Baskets That Caught the Wind

That dang ol’ Jethro (the Mogollon Monster) woke to a grey Morndas and a wind worrying the pines, and he declared it perfect leaf-catching weather. Mabel (a wood elf) wanted fallen leaves for her garden mulch, so Jethro lined the clearing with every empty basket at the Hearth and stood in the middle like a general.

Henny (the Hearth’s plump chicken) inspected the baskets, chose the finest one, and sat in it. She would not move. Not for corn. Not for compliments.

Then the wind gusted, leaves came tumbling in a brown spiral, and Flurry (a young ice-breathing dragon) tried to help by freezing the prettiest swirl in mid-air. It hung there, glittering, while Earl (an elk) walked straight into it and wore it like a very surprised hat.

By noon the baskets were full, Henny had ridden hers across the clearing twice without ever standing up, and Mabel had her mulch. Earl kept the leaf hat until supper. He said it was dignified. Nobody argued.
