Around the Holds · The Week Ahead

Grey Skies, Gay Doings: Trots, Markets and Music Fill the Weekend

By Muggy · Payson, Ariz.

Listen to this story

PAYSON — Fredas dawns grey over the Rim, friends of the Hearth, with a cool breeze worrying the pines — and the Holds are anything but quiet. Tonight Payson laughs at a free comedy show and learns the fine art of the holiday cheese board, Camp Verde gathers for word on the flood recovery, and Prescott is already dressed in Yuletide lights. Tomorrow the Turkey Trot takes Green Valley Park at dawn, and Sunday brings Beethoven to Sedona — all of it marching toward Thursday, when the Elks open their hall and set a free Thanksgiving table for the whole town.

The Horn Sounded from the Empty Ridge

Word comes this week from a Verde ferryman, and he swears it on his own oar. At dawn some days past, a hooded traveler stepped onto his raft and paid the crossing with a folded note — the very note, the ferryman now believes, that was left in the old tomb where the war horn should have hung.

Halfway over, a horn sounded from the far ridge, clear as a struck bell — though no horn hangs on that ridge, and no living soul stood there to blow it. The ferryman turned to ask his passenger what it meant, and found the raft empty but for a line of wet boot prints, leading nowhere, ending mid-plank.

The Hold’s rider reached the landing ten minutes later, of course. Ten minutes. The prints were already drying.

The Windsock That Pointed at Earl

That dang ol’ Jethro (the Mogollon Monster) decided Fredas that the Hearth needed a windsock, so he sewed one from a grain sack and raised it on a tall pole in the clearing. “Now we shall know the wind’s mind,” says he, proud as a jarl.

Trouble was, the wind’s mind seemed fixed on one subject. Every gust swung the sock around — and pointed it straight at Earl (an elk), whether Earl stood by the woodpile, the well, or clear across the clearing pretending not to notice. Henny (the Hearth’s chicken) rode the pole-top like a ship’s lookout and clucked at each swing. Flurry (a young ice-breathing dragon) helpfully froze the sock stiff mid-gust, pointing at Earl, so the whole Hold could admire the evidence.

“It accuses me,” said Earl, very stiff himself. But Mabel (a wood elf) settled it, as Mabel settles everything. “The wind isn’t pointing at you, Earl. You just always stand where the wind is going.” Earl has since taken to standing somewhere else. The sock points there now.
