Feast Week Opens Under Grey Skies, and the Holds Are Ready

By Muggy · Payson, Ariz.

Listen to this story

Hail, wayfarer, and pull up a bench — the mead hall is warm though the sky over the Rim hangs grey and cool this Tirdas morning! PAYSON — Feast week is upon the Holds, and there is honest work and honest fun in it: young archers loose their arrows in Payson this afternoon, neighbors in Cottonwood share out turkeys tomorrow morning so no table stands empty, and on Thursday the red rocks of Sedona ring with the Turkey Trot before the feast itself. A new coffee cart has also rolled into Payson this fall — Grindhead Coffee, run by David and Jennelle Brandt at the Tractor Supply on Highway 260 — and this edition raises a mug to it as Merchant of the Hold. Wrap up warm, traveler: 60 degrees is the day’s promise, with a low of 35 tonight.

Happening in the Holds Today

Listen to this story

  • Payson— Archery Practice — Payson’s young bowmen take the range at 902 W. Main Street from 4:15 to 5:15 PM today. It is a school practice, steady and serious in the best way: stance, aim, and the quiet thock of arrows finding their marks. Families and friends of the archers are the natural crowd for this one. If your own youngster dreams of splitting arrows like a hero of legend, this is the hour to watch how the craft is learned, one careful shot at a time.
  • Prescott— Benchrest Shooting at Wilhoit — Prescott Action Shooters hosts benchrest shooting on paper and steel today at 10 AM at its range in Wilhoit, about 30 minutes south of Prescott on Highway 89. Shooters settle in at the bench and chase tiny groups at distance, a patient and precise sport. It suits experienced shooters with their own gear and eye and ear protection. The drive down 89 through the pines is half the pleasure on a cool grey morning like this one.

The Days Ahead

Listen to this story

  • Wed, Nov. 25— The Great Turkey Giveaway — neighbors share out turkeys at 116 E. Pinal Street in Cottonwood at 9 AM, so feast tables across the Verde Valley stand full. It is the kind of plain, kind work a hold is proud of: show up, lend a hand or receive one, and give thanks. Arrive early, as birds go to tables while they last. Valley Dwellers driving up for the holiday are welcome in the line like anyone else.— Gila Dems Chair’s Office Hours — the Gila County Democrats hold office hours in Payson at 9 AM for anyone with a question, a concern, or a wish to help. A quiet, useful morning stop for civic-minded travelers. Bring your questions and a warm coat for the grey morning.
  • Thu, Nov. 26— Sedona Turkey Trot 5K — the 17th annual Thanksgiving morning run starts at 9 AM at Posse Grounds Park, 525 Posse Ground Road in Sedona, with a free Kids Dash at 8:45 AM. Runners and walkers of every age loop 3.1 miles under the red rocks and earn their feast honestly. Entry was listed at $45 for the 5K and $25 for ages 13 and under, and the registration page has shown the race as full, so check before you drive. Finishers take home a medal and a race shirt — fine loot by any bard’s reckoning.
  • Fri, Nov. 27— Magical Christmas Journey Begins — the Verde Canyon Railroad’s holiday train season kicks off today, with the train decked out for Christmas for its run through the canyon. It is a beloved Verde Valley tradition: lights, cocoa, and red-rock walls sliding past the windows. Book ahead, as holiday trains fill fast. A grand outing for guests who want their wonder served warm, in a seat, with no hiking boots required.
  • Sat, Nov. 28— Donuts with Democrats — the Democrats of Rim County gather at 110 E. Bonita Street in Payson from 10 AM to noon for donuts, coffee, speakers, and neighborly talk. The invitation is simple: share information, see friends and neighbors, and enjoy true community spirit. All the sweeter after feast day. Come hungry for both donuts and conversation.— Holiday Reserve Weekend at D.A. Ranch — D.A. Ranch Estate Vineyards in Cornville opens its holiday weekend today, with live music from 11:30 AM, Santa at 2 PM, and s’mores packages at 1901 N. Dancing Apache Road. Prescott Rhythm Section is among the acts named for the day. It is a vineyard holiday with a wink — wine, music, and Santa among the vines. This one is for the 21-and-over crowd; appoint a driver and enjoy.— Christmas at Town Center — Prescott Valley opens its holiday season at Town Center today from 3 PM, a family celebration to start the Christmas countdown. Expect lights, music, and a crowd of neighbors doing the same. A fine late-afternoon stop on the way home over the mountain. Bundle the little ones up against the evening chill.
  • Sun, Nov. 29— Holiday Reserve Weekend, Day Two — D.A. Ranch in Cornville carries its holiday weekend into Sunday, with afternoon live music from Sister & The Sun at the vineyard, 1901 N. Dancing Apache Road. A gentler second day to stretch the feast-weekend out properly. Wine, song, and winter sun when it shows itself. Grown-ups only, and a driver appointed, as ever.

Grindhead Coffee Payson · Payson

A cheerful coffee trailer newly rolled into Payson this fall, parked at the Tractor Supply store off Highway 260 and run by David and Jennelle Brandt as their semi-retirement venture. The beans hail from the Caribbean and are roasted in Tennessee, and every drink sails under a seafaring name — try the Salty Sailor, a salted caramel truffle latte, or the Captain Hook dark chocolate hazelnut mocha. A touch of sea-magic in the brewing, the bards swear, keeps every cup smooth as a calm harbor.

“even if you drink it black it doesn’t give you heartburn” — David Brandt, quoted in the Payson Roundup

At Tractor Supply, 510 E. Highway 260, Payson. Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 7 AM–2 PM; closed Sunday and Monday. Phone: not listed for the trailer — the host store answers at (928) 474-2829. Prices: moderate, most drinks around $7. Good to know: it is a trailer, so hard rain can close it for the day — check the shop’s Facebook page on a stormy morning. · their scrying-mirror page (other travelers call it a ‘website’)

The Tower That Rode an Elk

Tirdas dawned grey and cool, and Jethro (the Mogollon Monster) announced the Hold needed a lookout tower before lunch.

Not a tower of timber, mind you. A tower of acorns, balanced on Earl (an elk), who had made the mistake of standing still. Jethro stacked with great ceremony while Flurry (a young ice-breathing dragon) breathed one careful frost-puff at the base to steady it, and Henny (the Hearth’s chicken) supervised from a stump with the air of a building inspector who has seen it all.

The tower reached nine acorns high. Then Earl took one dignified step toward a sunbeam that was not there — it was overcast, Earl — and the whole tower rode two proud steps like a crown before cascading into Mabel’s herb path.

Mabel (a wood elf) sighed her professional sigh, declared it the finest mobile tower in the Hold, and charged Jethro one basket of gathered acorns for the cleanup. He paid gladly. Earl kept the top acorn, balanced between his antlers all day, just to show there were no hard feelings.
