Around the Holds · The Week Ahead

Flags Fly Over Payson as Thanksgiving Week Opens Across the Holds

By Muggy · Payson, Ariz.

Listen to this story

Hail, wayfarer, and pull up a bench by the hearth — the grey sky over the Rim this Sundas morning is no match for a week this full. PAYSON — On Wednesday, November 18, the Hold gathered for Rally Around the Flags, a ceremony some four years in the making that honors the flags of the United States, Arizona, and the armed services, and celebrates Payson’s standing as Arizona’s Flag Capital, with veterans and town leaders standing together under the banners. It is a fine way to open Thanksgiving week: today brings gratitude services and Sunday brunch in Payson, a winter farmers market and the opening concert of Chamber Music Sedona’s 44th season in Sedona, a merry gift market in Show Low, and string music in Prescott — and the days ahead hold a free Elks Thanksgiving dinner, a turkey trot, Santa’s workshop in Pine, and a star party in Camp Verde. Wrap up warm, traveler; the Holds are celebrating.

Happening in the Holds Today

Listen to this story

  • Payson— Sunday Brunch — 10 AM, 1504 W Country Club Dr. Brunch returns to Country Club Drive with mimosa and draft specials alongside the morning plates. It is a relaxed, sit-down start to a Sunday — good fuel before a walk, a Rim drive, or an afternoon of doing blessed little. Guests easing into a Rim Country day will find no finer first stop in the Hold.— A Grateful Heart! with Rev. Julianne Lewis — 10 AM at Unity of Payson, 904 N Beeline Hwy Suite E, and on Zoom, with Spiritual Soup at 11:15 AM. A Thanksgiving-week gratitude service in a small, welcoming room, where nobody stays a stranger for long. Stay after for soup and conversation — the fellowship is half the feast. A gentle, warm-hearted way to begin the holiday week.
  • Sedona— Sedona Community Farmers Market — winter Sundays, 11 AM–3 PM, in the Wells Fargo lot at 2201 W SR 89A. Regional growers and makers bring produce, fresh breads, honey, preserves, eggs, and prepared foods to the red-rock town. This is where Sedona shops for the Thanksgiving table, and the browsing alone is worth the drive. Come hungry and bring a sturdy basket.— Revolutionaries: Beethoven and America — 3–5 PM, Sedona Performing Arts Center, 995 Upper Red Rock Loop Rd. Chamber Music Sedona opens its 44th season with Canellakis, Hoopes, Sussmann, and Pajaro-van de Stadt playing Billings, Barber’s beloved Adagio, Beethoven’s Op. 135, and Dvořák’s “American.” The program marks America’s 250th year in grand style. An afternoon of world-class strings beneath the red rocks.— Women’s Descending & Drops Clinic — 10 AM, Sedona Bike Skills Park. Coaches from the Sedona Mountain Bike Academy lead women riders through steep slickrock rolls, ridgelines, and drops, one skill at a time. Sedona’s trails are famous and demanding, and this is the friendly way to meet them. Expect patient coaching, good company, and courage well earned.
  • Show Low— Merry Mountain Market — High Country Caring Fest — 11 AM–3 PM, Art Alliance of the White Mountains, 251 Penrod Rd. A handmade-gift market joined with a giving drive for neighbors, so your holiday shopping does double duty. Local makers fill the tables with gifts you will not find in any big-box hall. Browse, give, and leave with your list shorter and your heart fuller. Fine mountain-town merriment, by the Nine.— The Pink Farm Stand is OPEN — 12 PM until dark, at 45th Dr & Mogollon. A self-serve honor stand stocked with sourdough pepperoni balls and hand pies, scones, Thanksgiving cake pops, and mini cakes. You pay by cash, Venmo, or Zelle and take your treats on the honor system, the way small towns have always run. Worth the stop for the cake pops alone.— Kelley & Brent Rasmussen live — 1 PM, AZTEX Taproom. A Sunday-afternoon set from the Heber Ridge duo, all easy harmonies and mountain air. The taproom pours local brews while the music rolls, and nobody is in a hurry to leave. A fine way to spend the grey afternoon in good company.
  • Prescott— Academy of St Martin in the Fields Chamber Ensemble — 3 PM, doors at 2 PM, Jim and Linda Lee Performing Arts Center, 1100 E Sheldon St. The Yavapai Symphony Association presents one of the world’s most storied chamber ensembles on the Prescott stage. Tickets run through YSA at 928-776-4255 and at the door; clear bags only, so pack light. A rare treat for string lovers in the old capital.— Beginner Watercolor Painting with Upcraft Makers — 1–3 PM, with a second session 4–6 PM, Toothpick Art Studio, 371 Garden St Suite D. A gentle first step into watercolor, with all the guidance a new painter could want. You leave carrying a hand-painted bookmark or greeting card of your own making — just in time for holiday giving. No experience needed, only a willingness to splash.— R. Carlos Nakai — 5–7 PM, The Hazeltine, 208 N Marina St. The celebrated Native American flutist brings his haunting, peaceful sound to an intimate Prescott room at dusk. Few sounds suit a cool grey evening in the pines better than that cedar flute. Arrive early; a performance like this fills a small hall fast.

The Days Ahead

Listen to this story

  • Mon, Nov. 23— Ecumenical Thanksgiving Service — 1:30 PM, Unity of Payson, 904 N Beeline Hwy. Payson’s congregations gather under one roof to give thanks together before the holiday. All are welcome, whatever road brought you to town. A warm, unifying hour in the middle of the holiday bustle.— Family Aquagiving Splash Bash — 7–9 PM, Show Low Family Aquatic Center, and it is FREE. An evening of swimming, crafts, and a pie-eating contest to open Thanksgiving week with a splash. Bring the whole clan and a towel. Few feasts begin in a pool, and that is the fun of it.
  • Thu, Nov. 26— Payson Elks Free Community Thanksgiving Dinner — 11:30 AM–2:30 PM, Elks Lodge #2154, 1206 N Beeline Hwy. For more than 26 years the Elks have served a full Thanksgiving dinner to anyone in the community, with deliveries to the homebound. Nobody in this Hold eats alone on Thanksgiving unless they truly want to. Call 928-474-2572 to confirm times or arrange a delivery.— Sedona Turkey Trot 5K — 9 AM–12 PM, Posse Grounds Park, 525 Posse Ground Rd, with the Kids Dash at 8:45 AM. Earn the feast with a morning run beneath the red rocks: $45 for the 5K, $25 for the kids. Costumes are welcome and the cheer is plentiful. Valley Dwellers up for the holiday will find the cool Rim air a blessing.
  • Fri, Nov. 27— Santa’s Workshop at The Rusty Pine Cone — Santa 10:30 AM–3 PM, 3788 N AZ-87 in Pine, and again Saturday, Nov. 28. Shopping with the Elves, free photos with Santa, and free cocoa in a shop that smells of pine and cinnamon. It is the day-after-Thanksgiving done the small-town way — unhurried and merry. Bring the little ones and your gift list.— Live Music at Alcantara Vineyards — 12–4 PM, 3445 S Grapevine Way, Camp Verde, with Michael Kollwitz on Friday and Sky Conwell on Saturday, Nov. 28. The vineyard is open 11 AM–5 PM (closed Thanksgiving Day) for tastings on the lawn where the vines meet the Verde River country. Music, wine, and late-autumn sun — a fine Black Friday escape from every crowd.
  • Sat, Nov. 28— Star Party at Rockin’ River Ranch State Park — 7–10 PM, Camp Verde, free with park entry. The Astroverde Club sets up telescopes under some of the darkest skies in the valley of the Verde. Bring a red light and warm layers, and let the astronomers show you the heavens. Rim Country nights were made for this.— 29th Holiday Light Parade & Bonfire — parade at 6 PM from Cortez St to the Mile High Middle School field, bonfire 7–8:30 PM, all free. More than 40 lit floats roll through downtown Prescott, led by Santa himself, and the town gathers round the bonfire after. It is Prescott’s great holiday kickoff, bright enough to cheer the greyest week. Arrive early for a good spot on the curb.

LazyG Brewhouse · Prescott

A true mead hall for the modern road, LazyG pours its own house-brewed ales — HazyG IPA, Sunshine Blonde, and the Space 6 stout, plus a prickly pear cider — alongside gastropub cooking that punches far above tavern fare. The Reuben is the signature the whole town swears by, stacked tall and praised in review after review, with the BBQ Bacon Meatloaf Sandwich and Green Chile burger close behind. Save room for the bread pudding, traveler; the bards insist.

“By far the best food at any brewery. Whenever in Prescott I have to go and eat their Reuben.” — TripAdvisor review, “Best brewery ever!”, Oct 2026

The Sunshine Rehearsal

Grey skies hung over the Hearth all Sundas, so Jethro (the Mogollon Monster) declared a Sunshine Rehearsal — if the sun would not show up for the picnic, the picnic would practice without it.

Earl (an elk) took his basking pose on the blanket, chin high, eyes closed, soaking up sunshine that existed only in Jethro’s announcement. Gus (an orc baker) served hot cider “for authenticity,” though nobody could say what it authenticated. Henny (the Hearth’s chicken) wore a tiny scarf and sat on the picnic basket like she owned the summer.

Flurry (a young ice-breathing dragon) saved the day in his own fashion by breathing a small ice-crystal sun that hung sparkling over the blanket. “Close enough,” said Mabel (a wood elf), and sighed only twice. They rehearsed until the wind tugged the blanket loose and rolled it, Earl and all, two dignified feet across the clearing. The verdict: when the real sun returns, this Hold is ready.
