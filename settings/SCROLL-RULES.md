# The Payson Morning Scroll — Editorial Rules

Clay's rules for the Scroll, in one place. Clay edits this file on GitHub as needed;
Dude reads the current GitHub version before writing, building, or publishing each edition.
If a rule here conflicts with Dude's saved notes, this file wins until Clay says otherwise.

Companion list: **business-size-marks.md** — the Big/Local marks that decide whether a
business may be a Scroll merchant feature (Big = Gilded Pages directory only) or is
eligible (Local), town by town.

## If these files break — ask Clay, never guess
Clay edits these files himself, and an edit can go sideways. Before each edition Dude
fetches the GitHub copies of SCROLL-RULES.md and business-size-marks.md and checks them:
if a file cannot be fetched, is empty or garbled, has lost its headings/sections, a business
name cannot be matched to its Big/Local mark, or a rule is contradictory or unclear, Dude
STOPS that part of the work and asks Clay what happened — with tappable options — before
writing, choosing a merchant, or publishing. Dude never guesses at a broken rule, never
silently falls back to old saved notes, and never "fixes" Clay's edit on his own. A clean,
readable GitHub file always wins over saved notes; a broken one is a question, not a guess.

## People, names, and respect
- If an event lists a real person's name and/or a private-looking address, flag the item
  to Clay BEFORE publishing. He decides: include as-is, give replacement text, or drop it.
  Never redact or include it silently.
- Write about the Tonto Apache Tribe when appropriate — current-day or historical stories
  (85-acre reservation just south of Payson, smallest land base of any AZ reservation,
  created 1972; Te-go-suk, "Place of the Yellow Water"; descendants of the Dilzhe'e Apache;
  Mazatzal Hotel & Casino opened 1994, one of Payson's largest employers; known for
  beadwork and basketry). Absolute rule: never disrespect them — every story is fun and
  uplifting, putting no one down.

## Mission and voice
- The Scroll exists to help Mogollon's Hearth guests find interesting things to do around
  the Payson Hold. Big once-a-year events (fairs, rodeos) get a bigger spread as the date
  nears: early teases, then the featured illustrated box (what/where/when, admission, why go).
- Tone: light-hearted, hearty, and merry — a warm tavern-tale voice celebrating the town.
  The cheer lives in the voice, never in invented claims. Flavor never obscures the facts.
- Clay is the Publisher ("The Greybeards of High Hrothgar — Publisher & Proprietor");
  the paper is "Made Exclusively for the Mogollon's Hearth Guests". Dude is publisher/editor
  and proofreads every edition himself — Clay is never the copy editor.
- At Mogollon's Hearth, Clay is one of the Greybeards on sabbatical from High Hrothgar.
  Weave this in only very lightly when a natural moment arises; never announce it outright.
- "Hold" is singular for one town, "Holds" plural (e.g. "Two Fairs, One Hold"; "across the Holds").

## The Holds and geography
- Town order, nearest to furthest from Payson: Payson first, then Pine–Strawberry,
  Camp Verde, Sedona, Show Low, Prescott.
- Pine and Strawberry are a single Hold: twin villages ~4 mi apart on AZ-87 sharing one
  banner ("Rim Country"). Star Valley counts as part of the Payson Hold for merchants.
- Prescott is the westernmost Hold, Show Low the easternmost ("from Show Low to Prescott");
  Sedona is the northern hold, Mazatzal the southern.
- People from the Phoenix valley are "Valley Dwellers" — the same people as "lowlanders";
  the terms are interchangeable, used lightly and warmly. They are honored guests.
- Hold banners: small engraved heraldic banners from each town's real flag/symbol, flown
  automatically beside the town name. The American flag appears only for national
  holidays or national events.

## Skyrim calendar and language
- Dates use the Tamrielic reckoning (Morning Star = January … Hearthfire = September,
  Frostfall = October … Evening Star = December; Morndas = Monday … Sundas = Sunday),
  always with the real date alongside. Days Ahead bullets and prose date references carry
  the weekday: "Fredas, 2nd of Frostfall (Fri, Oct. 2)".
- The Tamrielic name is the proper name; the first use of a Skyrim month in each edition's
  prose gets a fresh, never-repeated playful jab at the lowlanders' name.
- There are no saloons in Skyrim — drinking places are mead halls, inns, or taverns.
- Anything modern that would be out of place in Skyrim is described with magic/fantasy
  instead of named plainly, followed by a brief parenthetical slightly-wrong whimsical name
  ("other travelers have called it a 'bagpipie'"). Keep it brief.
- Scotland is a distant mist-shrouded realm across the eastern sea; Scotsmen stay a small
  minority even at the Scottish faire (a bagpiper and a traveler or two) — crowds dress Nord.
  Cowboys get a short in-world gloss, not a long explanation.
- The merchant box's website link reads: "their scrying-mirror page (other travelers call
  it a 'website')".

## Merchants of the Hold
- One local business per edition; never repeat a featured merchant (kept in the featured log).
  The Scroll spotlight is for small, locally rooted businesses. The Big/Local marks in
  business-size-marks.md decide eligibility: Big = Gilded Pages directory only, never a
  Scroll feature. "Local" removes only the size exclusion — it does not by itself make a
  casino, hospital, or other institution a spotlight.
- Exactly ONE picture in the merchant box, always an old-timey engraving — never a real photo.
  Picture choice priority: 1) outside of the establishment, 2) inside, 3) the known sign,
  drawn from a drive-by perspective from Clay's house toward the place.
- When the paper sends guests somewhere, point out one or two REAL, verified sights along
  the drive from the Hearth, in old-timey voice, a line or two only. House phrasing:
  "...worth a rein-pull and a likeness snared in thy pocket-glass (other travelers have
  called it a 'celliephone')."
- The write-up carries a real verbatim review quote with source (never invented),
  address/hours/phone/price, one good-to-know, and a generous "why the Hearth sends you" line.

## Look and layout
- Old-parchment pages with rough torn edges (mixed scallops, notches, rounded bites, fibre
  jitter; darker toward the edges, lighter in the middle); every page tears differently.
- Stains: at most ONE coffee ring per edition, plus one-to-three other marks (water spot,
  ale ring, splash, damp blotch), different every day; stains sit behind the text.
- Boxed sections never split across pages. Keep boxes dense — no hollow blank space inside;
  blank space only where it helps reading. Pictures print in full, never cropped.
- Front-page hero: two smaller pictures side by side when two big events share page one.
- Weather box runs three across (chicken picture · forecast facts · "Overheard" column),
  and the "Overheard" line always carries its title (default "Overheard Last Night").
- The masthead dateline sections never break mid-phrase; wraps only between sections.
- Pages 2+ carry a running header (paper name left, short Skyrim + real date right) and a
  centered "· Page N ·" footer. Page 1 has no folio.
- The Dude's byline portrait prints as a circular badge on the same line as the front-page
  headline, "DUDE" arced along the bottom of the circle, "Payson, Ariz." centered under it.
  The portrait itself gets a subtle change every edition — never announced.
- When a mid-paper page would sit half empty, fill it first with a fresh engraving related
  to what's on that page (Days Ahead carries one, text wrapping around it) — pictures, local
  art, views of town, all old-timey — before considering any other filler. Do not pad with
  extra story filler.

## Illustrations
- Engravings only — never a real photo anywhere in the paper.
- Recurring or multi-day events get a FRESH illustration in every edition, never reused
  across days. Exceptions by design: the weather-box lodge scene, the Dude's portrait,
  and the hold banners.
- Mogollon Rim views are framed a little further away — more forest and life — with a
  natural few birds in the sky, never a swarm.
- Armor matches the Skyrim game's own designs (horned iron helmets and the like),
  never generic medieval knights.

## Weather box and the Overheard line
- The weather box shows the Hearth's lodge with the same plump chicken on the deck in that
  day's actual weather; only the sky, weather, and the chicken's gear change.
- The Overheard line is a FRESH saying every day — never repeated or closely resembled
  (kept in a log). The Dragonborn stars in it only rarely — never back-to-back days,
  roughly once a week at most — and is named when the gag is his near-miss. Default fare:
  everyday folk humor and tall rumors, creatures in the woods, mead-hall wisdom. When the
  Dragonborn does appear elsewhere, weave him in rarely, a little at a time.
- Payson's sawmill horns sound at 11:54 AM and 4:54 PM (Clay's firsthand times) — a living
  echo of the old mill's break signals. Never print a morning horn; never print a reason
  for the :54 as fact.

## Recurring features
- "Tales from the Treeline" closes every edition: ~150–220 words, brand new every time,
  with the named cast (Jethro the Mogollon Monster, Earl the elk, Gus the orc baker,
  Mabel the wood elf, Henny the chicken, Flurry the young ice-breathing dragon). Fire is
  completely off-limits in the tales. Premises are logged and never repeated.
- Every Friday: "Stories Around the Rim" — most weeks the Dragonborn's latest spectacular
  deed (the Hold always just misses him), some weeks another notable character or creature,
  tracked in its log.
- A light recurring theme may run across a few editions — about once a week or every couple
  of weeks, never back-to-back days, max two themes at once.
- The closing blessing rotates its menace daily by true calendar rotation:
  "May your [day] be free of dragons/wolves/bears/trolls/spiders/skeevers/giants/sabre cats, Clay".
- Lore bits on hand: Payson Airport is the iron drakes' landing-meadow (drake-barns, a
  striped wind-serpent, Crosswinds breakfast landings), woven in lightly when natural.

## Links and publishing
- Before publishing, check the CURRENT edition's links once and fix dead ones. Once a link
  is confirmed working, no repeated re-checks; old Scrolls are never re-audited.
  (Exception: the twice-yearly Gilded Pages refresh verifies every vendor-page link.)
- Corrections and rebuilds apply only to the current day's edition; older published
  editions stay as printed.
- Seasonal events must be verified against the organizer's own site (the Payson Farmers
  Market's 2026 season ended Sept. 12 — do not list it).
- Edition PDFs live on GitHub (claypnw/morning-scroll), outside the archive bundle.
  The contents search covers the full text of every edition; each publish adds the new
  edition's text to the index. The public table of contents is
  https://claypnw.github.io/morning-scroll/ and new editions are published there.
- The paper never contains Clay's personal calendar (the daybook stays in the private briefing).
- House time style: "8 AM–4 PM", "noon–4 PM", "8–10 AM" — never ":00". Tomorrow's forecast
  in the weather box is always labeled. Review attributions read "a Google review".
