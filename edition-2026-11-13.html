Pottery, Crafts, and a Full Fredas Across the Holds

Payson, Ariz.

Listen to this story

PAYSON — Hail, guest of the Hearth, and a fine grey Fredas to you — the clouds hang low over the Rim, the air is cool at sixty-two, and the Holds have filled this Friday with honest making. Potters open their festival at D.A. Ranch in Cornville, Santa’s craft fair fills the Elks Lodge in Show Low, holiday crafts spread out in Prescott, and here in Payson an estate sale and a cozy women’s gathering bookend the day. Wrap up warm, by the Nine, and go see what your neighbors’ hands have made.

  • Payson— Estate Sale — 703 N Manzanita Dr, Payson, today from 8:00 AM to 2:00 PM, and again Saturday. Everything must go: furniture, Christmas decorations, wall art, camping equipment, and more, from a house full of Rim Country living. Treasure hunters should come early, for the good pieces walk out first. Bring cash, a truck if you can, and a hunter’s eye. This is the kind of sale that furnishes a cabin in one morning.— Jesus & Jammies — 1100 W Lake Dr, Payson, today at 6:00 PM, $5 tickets or donations, and no one turned away for hardship. A women’s slumber-party gathering with pajamas, childhood snacks, friendship bracelets, games, and sweet conversation about faith and friendship. You do not need to know anyone to come — you are welcome exactly as you are. Bring your inner little girl and a friend who could use a night of joy.   • Camp Verde— Verde Valley Pottery Festival — First Pick Friday — D.A. Ranch Vineyards, 1900 Dancing Apache Rd, Cornville, today from 4:00 to 6:00 PM, $25. The festival’s early-access preview gives guests first choice of handmade ceramic work, with D.A. Ranch wine, local food trucks, craft beer, and live music along Oak Creek. Arizona potters bring bowls, mugs, and sculpture to over a hundred acres of cottonwood-shaded farmland. Saturday and Sunday are free, but Friday is when the best pieces still stand on the tables. Makers and collectors, this is your hour.

  • Show Low— Santa’s Winter Wonderland Craft Fair — Elks Lodge, 805 E. Whipple St, Show Low, today and Saturday, November 14, from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM each day. Arrive a little early and dress for the evening cool.

Mamajoe’s Italian Grill · Strawberry

A cozy Italian grill right on AZ-87 in Strawberry, serving the classics that keep locals and road-trippers coming back — legendary spaghetti, homemade meatballs, chicken parmesan, lobster ravioli, and wood-fired pizzas, all conjured in a small pine-country kitchen as if by a slow-simmered sauce spell. The signature thing to try is the spaghetti with homemade meatballs, the dish travelers cross Arizona to taste. Dinner only, Thursday through Sunday — plan for it like a quest, because it is one.

“Pack your patience, this isn’t Scottsdale and the food is fantastic.” — Review quoted in We3Travel

5076 AZ-87, Strawberry, AZ 85544. Hours: Thu 4–7 PM, Fri–Sun 4–8 PM; closed Mon–Wed. Phone (928) 476-3910. Prices: moderate. Good to know: it is a small restaurant and service runs at mountain pace — call ahead and do not be in a hurry.

A Note Where the Horn Should Be

Word comes down the high road from a pilgrim who swears it on his walking staff: the grey monks sent the hooded one to fetch an old war horn from a tomb of the first kings, and he went — ten minutes ahead of everyone, as always.

A barrow-keeper’s widow says the tomb door stood open, the traps all sprung and cold, and where the horn should have rested there lay only a folded note and a warm torch stub. A shepherd on the ridge swears he saw a distant hooded figure reading that very note by lantern light, then setting off not homeward, but toward the river town — as if the note, not the horn, were the true errand.

Mabel (a wood elf) doesn’t believe a word of it, naturally. But the pilgrim arrived at the Hearth a full ten minutes after the hooded one passed the gate, asking for directions he had already been given. By whom, traveler? By whom?

The Pinecones That Kept Vanishing

Fredas dawned grey and cool over the Hearth, the clouds hanging low, and Jethro (the Mogollon Monster) declared a grey day wanted brightening. He painted a dozen pinecones gold, hid them about the clearing, and announced a treasure hunt with a prize of Gus’s lemonade.

Henny (the Hearth’s plump chicken) won without moving. She had settled, early and unbothered, directly on top of the finest golden pinecone, and no clue Jethro read aloud could shift her. Earl (an elk) carried the collecting basket on his antlers with enormous dignity, gathering cone after cone, while Mabel (a wood elf) kept the tally and sighed only twice.

At eleven cones found, Jethro searched high and low for the last, until Henny stood up for a drink of water — and there it gleamed beneath her, warm as a fresh-laid egg. The hunt declared her champion, the lemonade was shared all round, and Henny, professional to the end, sat back down on her trophy.
